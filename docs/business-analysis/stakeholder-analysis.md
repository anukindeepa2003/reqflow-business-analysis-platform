# Stakeholder Analysis

## Simulation notice

This analysis describes fictional roles at the fictional company Apex Digital Solutions. No real stakeholder interviews, approvals, survey results, or organizational findings are claimed. The entries are hypotheses that would require validation in a real discovery process.

## Stakeholder register

Influence and interest use qualitative case-study estimates: low, medium, or high.

| Fictional stakeholder | Responsibilities | Information needs | Influence | Interest | Proposed elicitation techniques |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product Sponsor | Sets business direction and funds work | Scope, expected value, major risks, delivery status | High | Medium | Sponsor interview, objective workshop, milestone review |
| Lead Business Analyst | Owns requirement quality and analysis practices | Requirement status, history, dependencies, traceability, unresolved questions | High | High | Contextual interview, workflow observation, document analysis, prototype review |
| Project Manager | Coordinates scope, schedule, and delivery | Project progress, requirement distribution, risks, readiness signals | High | High | Interview, dashboard workshop, reporting artifact review |
| Software Engineer | Designs and implements product behavior | Clear requirements, acceptance criteria, priority, change rationale | Medium | High | Developer interview, requirement walkthrough, usability test |
| QA Engineer | Plans and executes verification | Testable criteria, linked tests, requirement coverage, status changes | Medium | High | Test-process workshop, artifact analysis, prototype walkthrough |
| Product Owner | Prioritizes desired outcomes and backlog | Business value, priority, scope boundaries, acceptance expectations | High | High | Prioritization workshop, story mapping, structured interview |
| System Administrator | Operates future environments | Configuration, access control, backup, logging, recovery needs | Medium | Medium | Technical interview, non-functional requirement workshop |

## Power-interest approach

- **Manage closely:** Product Sponsor, Lead Business Analyst, Project Manager, Product Owner.
- **Keep satisfied:** System Administrator, especially before deployment features are considered.
- **Keep informed:** Software Engineer and QA Engineer through requirement and prototype reviews.
- **Monitor:** No low-interest/low-influence role is defined in the current simulation. Additional roles may emerge during real elicitation.

This grouping guides communication effort; it does not represent real authority or approval.

## Initial information themes to validate

1. Where the current source of truth is believed to exist and how conflicts are resolved.
2. Which requirement attributes teams actually use and which create unnecessary overhead.
3. How priorities and statuses are interpreted by different roles.
4. What evidence QA needs to assess requirement coverage.
5. Which changes are important enough to record and how history should be presented.
6. What dashboard decisions users need to make; statistics should follow those decisions.

## Engagement principles

- Separate observed facts, stakeholder statements, assumptions, and analyst interpretations.
- Ask for examples and artifacts instead of treating an initial opinion as a requirement.
- Confirm terminology and decision rights before designing workflow states.
- Use prototypes to validate understanding, not as proof of approval.
- Record unresolved conflicts and decisions explicitly.
